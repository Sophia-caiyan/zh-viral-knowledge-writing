# Topic Adaptation

Use this reference whenever the user brings any topic and wants it converted into high-open-rate Chinese content.

## Input Diagnosis

Extract or infer these fields:

- Topic: the raw idea, concept, product, case, or draft.
- Reader: who should feel "this is about me".
- Platform: WeChat, Xiaohongshu, newsletter, video script, course note, or unspecified.
- Desired action: open, read to end, save, comment, share, trust, buy, join, reflect, or practice.
- Evidence: personal story, client pattern, research, public event, product experience, observation, or none.
- Risk: health, psychology, coaching, finance, law, children, trauma, current AI facts, or brand reputation.
- Source density: whether the user gave only a topic, a rough idea, a long draft, or source notes that must be preserved.

If the user only gives a topic, default to:

- Reader: knowledge workers or creators with practical anxiety.
- Platform: WeChat-style long post.
- Desired action: open, read, save, and share.
- Evidence: use hypothetical examples clearly marked as examples; do not imply they are real.

If the user gives a long draft, choose one of two modes:

- Light edit: preserve structure and voice. Use only when the user asks for polishing,润色, or keeping the original style.
- Free rebuild: preserve only the core thesis, facts, and constraints. Use when the user asks for 爆款改写, 完全重写, 咪蒙式结构, or says the current draft limits the rewrite.

In free rebuild mode, do not let the original opening determine the new opening. Build a new scene, conflict, and reversal from the topic.

## Convert Any Topic Into Reader Pain

Use this chain:

1. Topic: "What is this about?"
2. Reader situation: "Where does this show up in daily life?"
3. Hidden cost: "What is the reader losing without noticing?"
4. Misbelief: "What does the reader currently believe that keeps them stuck?"
5. Opposing force: "What should the article argue against?"
6. Useful promise: "What can the reader do after reading?"

For long drafts, compress the source into this 6-line brief before writing. Do not include the brief unless useful to the user.

Example:

- Topic: AI prompt writing
- Situation: User asks AI to write more but thinks less
- Hidden cost: Loss of judgment and voice
- Misbelief: Better prompts solve everything
- Opposing force: Outsourcing thinking
- Promise: Use AI as a thinking mirror instead of a ghostwriter

## Choose One Engine

### Emotional Judgment

Use when the topic involves injustice, social pressure, resentment, taboo, shame, or anger.

Structure:

1. Name the unbearable scene.
2. Show why it is not a small thing.
3. Identify the bad logic behind it.
4. Replace it with a cleaner value judgment.
5. Give the reader permission or action.

Best for: family pressure, workplace exploitation, gender norms, creator burnout, coaching boundaries, AI misuse.

### Practical Gain

Use when the reader wants tools, checklists, scripts, prompts, or decision rules.

Structure:

1. Start with a painful failed attempt.
2. Explain why common advice fails.
3. Offer 3-7 rules or steps.
4. Show before/after examples.
5. End with a simple practice.

Best for: AI workflows, writing, conversations, coaching questions, productivity, learning.

### Story Mystery

Use when the topic has a surprising case or person.

Structure:

1. Open with a strange fact.
2. Delay the explanation.
3. Reveal layers of motive, cost, or misunderstanding.
4. Land on a universal human truth.
5. End with a line that reframes the story.

Best for: profiles, interviews, product stories, career transformations, unusual events.

### Identity Mirror

Use when the topic can divide people into two types.

Structure:

1. Name the visible behavior.
2. Show the hidden identity behind it.
3. Contrast novice vs expert, avoider vs actor, anxious vs grounded.
4. Give a new identity script.
5. Offer a small behavior that proves the new identity.

Best for: growth, coaching, leadership, AI literacy, creator mindset.

## Angle Generation

Generate at least 3 angles:

- Rage angle: "What should the reader stop tolerating?"
- Shame-release angle: "What can the reader stop blaming themselves for?"
- Reversal angle: "What common advice is wrong?"
- Cost angle: "What is silently getting expensive?"
- Method angle: "What can the reader try today?"
- Identity angle: "Who does the reader become by accepting this frame?"

Pick the angle with the strongest overlap of:

- Emotional charge
- Reader relevance
- Novelty
- Evidence available
- Practical payoff

## Domain Adaptation

### AI Topics

Translate technical ideas into workflow consequences:

- Model capability -> what becomes easier/harder
- Prompting -> judgment and task design
- Agents -> delegation and verification
- Automation -> trust, control, and error cost
- Tools -> habits, taste, and decision quality

Avoid current product claims unless verified. If current facts matter, browse or ask the user for sources.

### Psychology Topics

Translate concepts into lived moments:

- Cognitive load -> "you are making too many decisions before the real work starts"
- Attachment -> "you call it love, but your body calls it danger"
- Self-compassion -> "you cannot shame yourself into safety"
- Boundary -> "you are not cold; you are finally stopping the leak"

Bound claims. Do not diagnose the reader.

### Coaching Topics

Make coaching concrete:

- Bad question -> loop
- Better question -> choice
- Reflection -> pattern
- Practice -> next behavior
- Boundary -> contract

Avoid guru certainty. Good coaching content should feel clear, not controlling.

## Minimum Useful Output

For any topic, produce:

- Reader pain in one sentence
- Opposing force in one sentence
- Counterintuitive claim in one sentence
- Practical promise in one sentence
- 3 angles
- 8-12 title candidates
- 1 recommended title/subtitle pair
- Opening paragraph or outline
