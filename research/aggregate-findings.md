# Aggregate Findings

These findings summarize reusable patterns from a private Chinese viral long-form article corpus. They are written as writing heuristics, not as guaranteed formulas.

## Research Snapshot

- Metadata rows inspected: 1111.
- Local PDF matches: 911.
- Readable body pool: 719.
- Deep study sample: 116.
- Median article length in the study sample: about 2547 Chinese characters.
- Median paragraph density: about 26 Chinese characters per paragraph.
- Median article contained about 14 questions, 16 dialogue or comment cues, and 68 direct reader/self references.

## Core Pattern

The strongest transferable pattern is:

1. Find a reader's private pressure.
2. Name the bad logic, hidden cost, or false comfort behind that pressure.
3. Convert the abstract topic into a recognizable scene.
4. Give a defensible reversal.
5. Deliver practical value after the reader feels seen.
6. End with a sentence the reader can use to explain themselves.

## Title Findings

High-pull titles usually combine at least two of these:

- Reader implication: "你", "我", "我们", or a precise identity.
- Conflict: someone is wrong, something is unfair, a belief is broken.
- Reversal: not X but Y.
- Concrete stake: money, time, dignity, love, opportunity, judgment, or exhaustion.
- Emotional verb: afraid, stuck, tired, wasting, seeing through, letting go.
- Social currency: the title lets readers express a stance by sharing.

Weak titles usually fail because they are abstract, overpromise, lack a specific reader, or cannot be delivered by the available material.

## Opening Findings

Effective openings rarely start with definitions. They usually start with:

- A recognizable scene.
- A confession or discovery.
- A dilemma.
- A contradiction.
- A public event that quickly becomes about the reader.

The first screen should show a person or situation, a visible conflict, and a reason to care.

## Body Findings

The body works best when emotion and usefulness alternate:

- Scene.
- Conflict.
- Reader recognition.
- Counterintuitive claim.
- Evidence, story, or supplied material.
- Mechanism.
- Method or action.
- Value judgment.
- Ending line.

Short paragraphs help mobile reading, but sentence-by-sentence chopping cannot replace information progress. Each section should add a new reason, example, distinction, or action.

## Ending Findings

Strong endings usually do one of four things:

- Give permission.
- Draw a boundary.
- Name the real lesson.
- Offer a small next action.

An ending that merely summarizes the outline has low share value.

## What Not To Copy

Do not copy a person's identity, biographical position, private anecdotes, habitual catchphrases, insults, or exact emotional performance. Copy only the transferable mechanics: scene, tension, reversal, proof, usefulness, and shareable judgment.
