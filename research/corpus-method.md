# Corpus Method

This repository publishes distilled writing methods, not the source article corpus.

## Data Summary

The private research corpus used for this skill contained:

- 1111 article metadata rows with title, date, position, reads, likes, and author fields.
- 911 rows matched to local PDF files.
- 719 PDFs with at least 1500 extractable Chinese characters.
- 116 readable articles selected as a stratified study sample across topic category, year, and engagement tier.

The raw PDFs and extracted full text are not committed to this repository.

## What Was Studied

The research focused on transferable structure:

- Title length, punctuation, pronouns, numbers, question marks, and emotional words.
- Title hook combinations.
- Article length, paragraph density, question density, dialogue cues, and reader address.
- Opening patterns in the first 300 characters.
- Ending patterns in the last 300 characters.
- Article archetypes: emotional judgment, relationship truth, workplace/money, identity reversal, growth, story mystery, practical how-to, and interaction formats.

The purpose was not to preserve any creator's exact voice. The purpose was to understand why some Chinese long-form articles feel concrete, emotionally charged, and easy to share.

## Public Boundary

The public skill includes:

- Aggregated findings.
- Writing workflows.
- Synthetic examples.
- Evaluation cases.
- A deterministic lint script.

The public skill does not include:

- Full source articles.
- Large excerpts from source articles.
- Private metadata beyond aggregate counts.
- Any instruction to copy a living writer's exact persona.

## Research Limits

The corpus is useful for discovering patterns, but it does not prove universal causality. Engagement data can be capped or distorted by distribution, platform timing, topic heat, and audience fit. Treat the findings as strong heuristics to test against a user's own audience.
